    Stage 2 · 8 minutes

    Form, meaning, use

    Three compact tables and five contrast pairs. Read this after the entry check, with your own error pattern in mind — you are looking for the row that explains what you just got wrong.

    The one rule to carry into the exam

    If you say when, use the past simple. If you cannot say when — or when it does not matter — use the present perfect. Nothing else lifts tense accuracy faster at this level, because the single most common error in Band 5.5 Speaking is a present perfect glued to a finished-time marker.

    Contrast pairs

    Both sentences in each pair are correct English. Only the meaning changes. This is the level of control Band 7 asks for — not avoiding errors, but choosing between two accurate forms on purpose.

    Why this is harder if your first language is Turkish

    Turkish marks duration with -den beri plus a present tense: 2020’den beri burada yašıyorum maps word-for-word onto “I live here since 2020”. English needs a perfect form there. Turkish also has no single form that matches the English perfect, so the two Turkish past markers (-dı and -mıș) both tend to come out as the past simple. Neither of these is carelessness — they are predictable transfer patterns, and they respond quickly to targeted practice because there is a rule to hold on to rather than a feel to develop.

    Stage 3 · 20 minutes

    Scaffolded practice

    Five stages, each removing a little support: you notice the signal, then choose the form, then repair a broken sentence, then produce the form from scratch, then apply it inside a Speaking exchange. Twenty-eight items. Check each one as you go — feedback that arrives immediately is what makes the pattern stick.

    1

    Notice — what does the time expression force?

    No verbs to conjugate yet. Just read the time expression and decide which period it describes.

    2

    Choose — select the form

    Academic contexts across six topic areas. Several items have two gaps, because in real speech the two tenses sit in the same sentence.

    3

    Repair — fix the sentence

    Each sentence contains one tense error. Type the corrected version. Where more than one repair is legitimate, all accepted answers are shown when you check.

    4

    Produce — build the form yourself

    No options to choose from. Write the verb phrase that fits, using the verb in brackets. Contractions are accepted.

    5

    Apply — hold the frame across turns

    This is where Band 5.5 usually slips. The first answer is fine; the follow-up loses the time frame. Choose the response that keeps control.

    Stage 4 · 10 minutes

    IELTS transfer — Speaking Parts 1, 2 and 3

    Speak these aloud and record yourself if you can. The point is not to be error-free; it is to hear whether the frame holds when you stop thinking about grammar and start thinking about content.

    Keep the three demands separate

    This page trains Speaking only. The same contrast behaves differently elsewhere in IELTS Academic: Writing Task 1 is dominated by the past simple, because the data carries dated, finished periods; Writing Task 2 uses the present perfect mainly to establish a trend before an argument shifts to the present or future. Do not carry Task 1 report language into Part 3 — it sounds rehearsed, and it costs you on Fluency and Coherence.

    Part 1 — familiar topics

    Answer each in four to six sentences using the AREA shape (answer, reason, example, add). Watch the tense in the follow-up, not just the first sentence.

    1. Where did you study before this course, and what did you enjoy about it?
    2. Has the way you study changed since you started preparing for this exam?
    3. What is the most useful thing you have learned in the last year?
    4. Did you use technology much in your classes at school?

    Part 2 — long turn

    Full task, full timing: one minute to prepare, one to two minutes to speak.

    Describe a change you have made in the way you study.

    You should say:

    • what the change was
    • when and why you made it
    • how you study now compared with before

    and explain what difference this change has made to your results.

    1 minute preparation, then 2 minutes speaking.

    Part 3 — change over time

    These are the questions that expose tense control, because each one mixes a period running up to now with specific finished events. Aim for one clean movement between frames per answer — open in the present perfect, drop to the past simple where you name a year, come back.

    1. How have study habits changed in your country over the past twenty years?
    2. Some people say universities have become too focused on employment. What is your view?
    3. What effect has online learning had on the way students work together?
    4. Do you think the changes we have seen in education so far will continue?

    4 · Turkish L1 note

    Codes T3 and T4 are where Turkish-speaking learners cluster. Turkish expresses duration up to now with -den beri plus a present tense, so “I live here since 2020” is a structural transfer, not inattention — name it that way. Turkish also lacks a form corresponding to the English perfect, so both -dı and -mıș pasts surface as the past simple, which is why the default error direction is under-use of the present perfect, with over-use appearing only in the specific T1 case where a learner has been taught the perfect and applies it enthusiastically to last year. Frame every one of these as a predictable pattern with a rule attached, never as a personal weakness — it de-personalises the error and keeps the learner working on it.

    5 · Calibration notes

    • Load: one grammar contrast, no new vocabulary set, one strategy (frame continuity across turns). This is deliberately at the cognitive-load ceiling for a 45-minute session; do not add a second grammar point.
    • Standard used: British usage throughout, which is what IELTS examiners are trained on. Where American English tolerates the past simple with just, already and yet, items avoid that grey zone entirely rather than penalising the learner for a legitimate variant — every scored item is unambiguous in both varieties.
    • Progression: 5.5 to 7.0 in Grammatical Range and Accuracy is roughly a 200–250 guided-hour journey across all four criteria. This lab addresses one high-frequency accuracy leak within that. Say so to the learner; a lab that promises a band is a lab they will stop trusting.
    • Homework: the personalised panel deliberately issues one drill, not six. Calibrate to about 80% confidence that it gets done.
